# Briefing research

Run `npm ci` here, then `node --test test/*.test.mjs`.
From the deployment root, `node --use-env-proxy .research/scripts/research-briefing.mjs --deployment` refreshes briefing.json, briefing-history.json and the embedded snapshot.

Discovery reads publisher RSS/Atom feeds and AP/Business Insider section pages. A round-robin queue reads up to 240 unique articles, without rejecting them for a low headline interest score. Section discovery never supplies an invented publication date. Only dated, readable source pages qualify.

Ranking uses the article lead alongside the headline, retains the user's 35/30/20/15 weights, discounts unsupported capability claims, rejects routine statements and AI growth predictions, and clusters duplicate sanctions-reversal coverage. This remains deterministic scoring, not an LLM editor or exhaustive web search. Availability and rejection diagnostics are included in briefing.json. Direct Reuters pages may deny requests; accessible syndicated reporting can be discovered through Investing.com. Do not bypass access controls.

Explanations are attributed source excerpts. Science excerpts search for a contiguous passage with findings instead of blindly taking atmospheric introductions. No invented paraphrases or dates. Five real articles remain visible with older coverage explicitly labeled when necessary.

The deployed index.html contains a bundled copy of public/news.js; update both when changing shared ranking. Preserve the rest of the HTML, especially browser storage key daymark:v1 and personal feature handlers. No credentials or personal calendar data belong in public files.

## Personal five-slot layout

The active layout is `nyt-interests-us-world-v1`: the first item in NYT's official HomePage RSS feed; two personal-interest reports; one US report; one world report. `selectPersonalBriefing` creates the edition. `publishedPersonalBriefing` preserves its ordering across browser visits, refreshes, failures and check-in boundaries. Do not pass a published fixed-slot edition back through global ranking.

Only topic preferences are in source; do not publish the user's biography. Preferences cover EE/CS, embedded electronics, battery/materials work, AI, college access and major scholarships, ROTC, aviation/space and related interests. Mere college names, AI-related criminal scandals, speculative jobs commentary, and sponsored content do not qualify as personal relevance. Urgent eligibility, cost or deadline changes can replace slots 2–5, explicitly labeled. NYT lead is never displaced by scoring.

NYT RSS summaries may be used when the full NYT article is unavailable, labeled feed-only. If the official feed is unreachable, keep a previous NYT lead or explicitly label an alternative `NYT unavailable`; never relabel another publisher. The NYT lead is allowed to remain unchanged. Other slots retain story-history filtering. The hourly catch-up retries NYT feed outages.

Run `node --test .research/test/*.test.mjs` from the deployment root. `node .research/test/personal-browser.mjs` verifies fixed slots, feed-only labels, bookmarks, refreshes, next-window carryover and offline behavior in Chromium. `RESEARCH_REVIEW_PATH=/tmp/candidates.json` optionally saves all checked candidates for local review; do not publish that file. No API key or additional paid service is needed for this feed-based implementation.

Personal slots also target a high-school reading level. `studentAccessibility` rewards familiar devices, clear outcomes and student relevance, and penalizes prerequisite terminology in a headline/lead. Dense professional explainers and abstract research do not qualify merely because they match EE/CS interests. `studentPresentation` prefers a readable publisher summary when available and uses evidence-guarded plain-language descriptions for supported concrete outcomes. Do not claim a fix or a measurement unless the source data contains it. Save the original headline, source link and attribution. Other news slots retain their normal selection rules.

National and world slots have a separate scope check: `publicNewsScope`. A location mention and a publisher's World/Technology section label cannot establish public-news eligibility. Slots 4 and 5 require a reported public-affairs development with substantial public scope; engineering explainers, arts/research features and small local incidents cannot fill them through either normal ranking or fallback. `personalBriefingProblems` runs before any briefing/history/HTML writes and rejects incorrect slot assignments, duplicates and inaccessible personal cards. A truly urgent personal eligibility/cost/deadline change remains an explicit allowed override. During source outages, retain correctly typed earlier reporting; never relabel an unrelated feature to fill a default slot.


## Private device sync

The deployed `sync-client.js` and `sync-core.js` are optional ES modules loaded after the dashboard starts. Failure to load or connect cannot stop local saves. Configure a Supabase project using `sync-setup.html` and `supabase-setup.sql`; the latter enables and forces row-level security and merges entries atomically under an account-row lock. Authentication is Supabase email/password with email confirmation, token refresh and password recovery. Only a project URL and publishable/legacy anon key are browser configuration; secret/service-role keys are rejected. `sync-config.json` intentionally contains no project until the owner connects one. Settings can copy a setup link containing the public connection values for another device.

Personal lists use stable item IDs; exercise uses independent check-in/checkbox keys; deleted records retain tombstones. Hybrid timestamp versions with deterministic device-ID ties merge offline edits and avoid replacing an entire device snapshot. Same-item simultaneous edits resolve by that version order, rather than combining prose. Offline journal data and the original `daymark:v1` state persist locally. Provider caches, UI visibility, account credentials, and passwords are not included in the synchronized personal payload or dashboard exports. Session tokens are held only in a separate browser auth key; personal data is never committed to GitHub. A pre-sync backup is available in Settings. Polling runs every 15 seconds only while visible, on return/online, and after an edit; unchanged responses omit the history payload.

Tests: `node --test .research/test/*.test.mjs` includes actual PostgreSQL (PGlite) RLS/account isolation, merge, deletion and validation checks. `node .research/test/sync-browser.mjs` exercises independent desktop/mobile browser contexts against that database merge function, with a mocked Supabase authentication transport. It covers preexisting history, bidirectional edits, offline/reload recovery, completion, deletion, token refresh, isolation, sign-out and exports. A real hosted project still needs the one-time owner setup and a real sign-in check before claiming hosted sync is active. `CHROMIUM_PATH` can select the browser executable; default is `/usr/bin/chromium`.


## October news reliability repair

Selection excludes previously published stories even when two runs share the same Eastern check-in key. The NYT first-feed-item rule is preserved; an unchanged lead is labeled and is not counted as new. Source-copy clustering also uses canonical URLs, cited primary DOIs, and specific shared event terms/actions. Opinion, routine corporate compliance/adoption, investment chatter, career profiles and image-of-the-day items cannot fill personal science/tech slots. A university affiliation does not create college/admissions relevance. Explicit event dates (including records surpassed earlier in the month) outrank a new article publication timestamp.

All selected links, including archive fallbacks, are fetched and parsed again immediately before publication. Dead, unreadable or subscription-only non-NYT pages are replaced from the checked candidate pool. The explicitly requested NYT lead can retain its official public-feed summary during publisher access restrictions, with a visible subscription/access label; it is not represented as a freely readable full article. Generated image captions, including “image generated by ChatGPT,” suppress the image.

The static website refresh button retrieves the latest published JSON without an unrelated discarded RSS check. It explicitly reports when no newer edition has been published; a browser fetch timestamp is not a new briefing. Scheduled generation still targets 7 AM/7 PM Eastern, with off-peak half-hour catch-up triggers. GitHub cron may start late. The workflow must also remain on main, because schedules run from the default branch. A complete edition with an unchanged NYT lead alone is not churned hourly.

Regression coverage: `news-reliability.test.mjs` and `news-refresh-browser.mjs`; verify actual hosted JSON after the source-push research job completes. Personal state and Supabase sync modules are unchanged.

Source-push jobs now use the edition/quality guard rather than needlessly regenerating a just-published valid briefing during a UI correction. The guard checks the actual five-slot contract, and any invalid current card forces repair; scheduled new Eastern check-ins still regenerate, and workflow_dispatch can force a new research run. Hypothetical would/could/might headlines without a confirmed public action cannot fill the national/world slots. A correction records repairedAt separately from the original research time; refresh compares that revision as well as the original fetchedAt. New-story counts in the UI use the browser’s actual reading history instead of calling the same four cards new after every refresh.


## Reusable editorial selection and live search

Every generation now discovers reporting through eight broad live news searches, publisher sections and RSS feeds, plus up to six searches based on promising headlines found during that run. Search snippets only nominate URLs from allowed publisher hosts; every selected non-NYT report must still pass original-page, date, readability, and prepublication link checks. Search failures leave the independent feeds/sections available. The reading budget is shared by publisher rather than by the number of feeds a publisher exposes. Additional primary institutional coverage and accessible technology publishers are included.

`editorialAssessment` requires evidence of a result, tradeoff, practical consequence or public action. The score uses 35% surprise, 30% consequence, 20% novelty and 15% reader-interest relevance; a category label or incidental topic keyword cannot qualify a story. It is a transparent heuristic, not a human or language-model editor. It records matched evidence and rejection reasons for review. Personal slots reject weather disruption, routine appointments, background explainers, campaign appearances and empty/live results coverage. Existing scope checks still enforce NYT / two personal science-tech stories / U.S. / world. Strong unseen reports up to 72 hours old may beat weak newer items with a freshness penalty and the visible earlier-report label; older archive reports are last-resort coverage, never represented as new.

Source extraction preserves split article bodies, removes author biographies and publisher footers, checks direct Nature/Science paper links via Crossref, and selects source sentences explaining the lead development and its mechanism or consequence. Generic wording changes replace article-specific hand-written summaries. Unknown event dates remain labeled; an institution's announcement date is not a claim about when its experiment occurred.

A complete valid edition remains stable until the next 7 AM/7 PM Eastern check-in, even if one item is carried, earlier, or a saved NYT lead during an outage. Hourly catch-up previously consumed good stories by regenerating complete editions repeatedly. `EDITORIAL_VERSION` triggers a single policy upgrade; an invalid/short edition can still be repaired and a manual dispatch can force generation. GitHub schedules can arrive late.

`editorial-policy.test.mjs` checks these rules across consecutive editions, negative examples, publisher diversity, trusted search URLs, split source extraction, direct paper links, generic summaries and stable catch-up behavior. `research.editorialAudit` and each selected story's `editorial` object expose why candidates ranked as they did without relying on hardcoded article URLs.
