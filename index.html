Sources & selection

1: the first story in NYT’s official HomePage RSS feed. 2–3: approachable, relatable reporting connected to your interests, written for a curious high-school student. Clear outcomes, familiar examples, and practical relevance come before specialist terminology. 4: substantial U.S. public affairs or national events. 5: consequential international reporting. Both slots require an actual reported public event with broad impact. Previously published stories are excluded even within the same morning/evening check-in, except for a labeled unchanged NYT lead or genuine lack of new reports. Selected links are checked immediately before publication; non-NYT subscription-only pages and opinion columns are rejected. Important personal eligibility, cost, or deadline changes can replace a default slot and are labeled. Publisher order is preserved for the NYT lead; that lead may stay unchanged between visits. Other slots favor new developments within 24–48 hours and avoid repeated stories. Earlier reporting and saved coverage are labeled when sources are quiet or unavailable. Feed-only NYT summaries are explicitly marked when full articles are inaccessible. Personal-interest notes explain relevance without claiming eligibility or an effect that the reporting does not establish.

      THE GOOD ADDS UP

      Your little collection

      A calmer kind of dashboard.

      Your morning and evening reset: a little perspective, a little movement, and something good to keep.

      What works now

      Exercise tracking and a streak for consecutive completed check-in windows, a personal good-moments journal, manual calendar events, school-break dates, running records, a separate no-YouTube streak, a daily Duolingo reminder, a challenging daily vocabulary word, class to-do lists with time estimates, and a twice-daily quote, joke, and fun fact.

      Live connections

      News from BBC, NPR, Ars Technica, The Verge, science publishers and college reporting, public Google Sheets work when their providers can be reached. The downloaded dashboard uses a public-feed service for news and Google’s own API for the Sheet; those services can have delays or outages. Failures and old data are labeled. News selection uses automated weighted scoring and source-date checks. It keeps five real reports visible: new reporting first, then clearly labeled catch-up or previously covered stories when needed. Event dates are only marked confirmed when supported by the source.

      About the schedule

      A new check-in opens at 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. Eastern. An open page checks news and your Sheet within 30 seconds of each 7 AM / 7 PM reset; background source research may take a few minutes to finish. Colors switch to dark mode at 7 PM and back to light at 7 AM; reopening catches up. Exercise gets a fresh window. Your journal is kept and the quote, joke, rabbit hole, and soft color palette shuffle at each check-in. Vocabulary changes daily. Running and no-YouTube logs are kept. The server also checks feeds while running. This local version does not send notifications or run when its host is stopped. A hosted deployment is needed for always-on access.

      Your privacy

      The Duolingo reminder is self-reported and links to your profile; it does not read your Duolingo account. Personal entries stay in this browser’s local storage. No account, analytics, or cloud sync is included. Use Settings to back up your data.
